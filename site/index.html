Read in the car without feeling sick

Moving Dots draws small dots along the edges of your screen, on top of any app, that move with the vehicle. What you see then matches what you feel.

How it works

Your body feels the ride

Your inner ear senses every acceleration, brake and turn, but your eyes see a screen standing still. That mismatch is what makes you feel sick.

The dots move with it

Accelerating pushes the dots down, braking pushes them up, and turns push them sideways: the direction your body feels.

The screen stays free

The dots stay at the edges, and touches pass through to the app you're using.

Features

  • Quick Settings tile to turn the dots on and off with one tap
  • Adjustable sensitivity, dot size, opacity, count and color, with a live preview
  • Dots on the side edges only, or also at the top and bottom
  • Optional ride ratings (a 0–10 symptom scale) to see whether the dots help you
  • Optional notification
  • Tiny: under 100 KB without Google Play services

Both need Android 12 or later and can be installed side by side.

Private by design

The app has no internet permission: nothing ever leaves your phone. Motion sensors only run while the dots are on and the screen is on.

Use it as a passenger, never while driving. Evidence for visual motion cues is still limited, and they help some people more than others: the ride ratings are there to check whether they work for you.
